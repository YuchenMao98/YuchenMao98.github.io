Research

Better decisions from data

  • Planning ahead

    Before demand is known

    • Which patterns matter?

      Use relationships across products, locations, and time to inform production and inventory plans.

    • What if forecasts are wrong?

      Study how prediction errors affect decisions and how to reduce their impact.

  • Adapting over time

    As new information arrives

    • How can we plan further ahead?

      Estimate future costs and improve decisions without examining every possible future scenario.

    • What do new data reveal?

      Explore how new observations can guide decisions when some information remains hidden.

My research lies at the intersection of operations research and statistics. I study how businesses can use data to plan production, inventory, and supply networks when demand is uncertain. These questions connect my work on learning from data, accounting for prediction errors, and making decisions over time.

Technical topics

The common framework is contextual optimization: using available information to guide decisions under uncertainty.

Planning ahead covers two-stage and bilevel problems, with work on regression with correlated uncertainties, latent factors, time-series forecasting, and bias correction through Neyman orthogonality and double machine learning.

Adapting over time covers multistage problems, including value estimation, stochastic approximation, and multi-index Monte Carlo, alongside an interest in partially observable Markov decision processes.

Value Estimation estimates the expected cost associated with a given first-stage decision, with later decisions optimized as uncertainty unfolds.

Stochastic Approximation studies gradient estimation and first-stage optimization, using the estimated gradients to improve the initial decision.
