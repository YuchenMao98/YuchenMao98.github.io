Research

Research statement

My research lies at the intersection of operations research and statistics. My goal is to develop data-driven methods that turn uncertain, heterogeneous, and time-dependent information into reliable operational decisions. I combine optimization with statistical and econometric tools, with an emphasis on performance guarantees and applications in business operations.

More specifically, my research focuses on three connected areas:

  • Optimization under uncertainty
  • Econometrics and forecasting
  • Business applications

Optimization under uncertainty

I am interested in stochastic programming, robust optimization, and distributionally robust optimization. These frameworks provide a principled way to make decisions when the underlying distribution is only partially observed or must be learned from limited data.

My work studies how statistical information can be incorporated into optimization models while retaining tractability and out-of-sample reliability.

Econometrics and forecasting

I use causal inference, time-series models, and predictive methods to learn the structure of uncertain operational environments. I am particularly interested in settings with latent factors, correlated outcomes, heterogeneous variability, or decision-dependent data.

A central theme is the integration of prediction and prescription: forecasts are evaluated not only by statistical accuracy, but also by the quality of the decisions they support.

Business applications

My applied interests include supply-chain analytics, production planning, and service operations. I study how firms can design reliable plans and allocate capacity when demand and other operational inputs are uncertain.

Current projects consider predictive planning for supply networks and integrated production-and-service systems.
